# Review — applier 0.17

## Why

One finding from the first light on the bench, 2026-10-13 (FL-GAP-6 in the first-light acceptance
record). A mains Zigbee plug, adopted, was unplugged until its bridge reported it `available: false`,
and the applier read it `dead`. When it was plugged in again, its own unprompted reports brought it
back within 30 ms, carrying `on`, unchanged, and five keys the applier had never held: settings the
plug had never reported before (a countdown, its power-outage memory, its indicator mode, its child
lock, its button mode). Applier 0.16 reports a first value arriving live from a running bridge instance
on an adopted device as `external` (GA-EVT-1), so each was a person's change. Each is a key one of the
plug's actions sets, so the steward took a person's hold on the plug for an hour (steward GA-LEASE-2),
and the owner's rule switching the plug off was `refuse(leased)` for that hour. No person had touched
the plug's settings; the device was saying what it holds as it came back.

The maintainer ruled (2026-10-13): a key's first value that arrives with the device's return from
`dead` is the device's word, `device`, and takes no lease; not the return from `stale`; only first
values. Two wider fixes were logged in the workshop's backlog, not made: a setting's first value as
`device` whenever it arrives (it would also cover a power cut shorter than the time to `dead`), and a
plug whose power-on behaviour changes its held `on` at the return, which reads `external` under every
option.

## Changes

Round 1 reviewed a narrower rule (a first value with a device's return from a bridge-reported `dead`,
within a 5 s window). All three readers found that it missed the commonest case: a whole-house power cut
restarts the applier and the bridge, the plug rejoins after the snapshot without ever reading `dead`, and
its never-held keys arrive live, `external`, and lease it; also an applier restart during an unplug, an
unplug shorter than the time to `dead`, and adapter devices. On those findings the maintainer replaced
the rule (2026-10-13): every first value is the device's word. Round 1's findings and the earlier rule
stay below under *Readers* for the record; the table here is what round 2 reads.

| Rule | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| GA-EVT-1: a key's first value, the applier holding none before, is `device` wherever it arrives, a live report on an adopted device included, unless an apply's correlation gives it that apply's; the five arrival cases (load, snapshot or new instance, new route, not adopted) no longer decide the cause | **looser** (fewer `external` changes, so fewer person's holds) | First light: a plug's power-up leased it for an hour and refused the owner's rule; the narrower rule of round 1 did not survive restarts, short outages or adapters |
| GA-EVT-1: a held key's changed value is `external`, at a device's return from `dead` as at any time | **stricter** (stated, unchanged in effect) | Keeps a wall switch's flick and a person's change a person's |
| *Causes*: the rule's prose; the cost stated: a person's real first act on a key the applier never held on that device takes no lease; the plugin server's known gap removed, now closed; the checks on a real installation now that a held key's change after a restart, a power cut or a route move is still told from a first value | **looser**, prose of the rule above | The same |
| The gloss of `device` ("never a person's") becomes "no hand can be told from it", here and in `CONTEXT.md` | **moved** (text) | It now knowingly covers a person's rare first act |
| *Compatibility*: 0.16 and 0.17 change causes, said without "from a retained status" | **moved** (text) | The old wording contradicted the retain-flag rule |
| Negative subjects: `devices-a-live-press` withdrawn (it describes the rule now); `externalises-a-snapshot-value` and `externalises-a-rerouted-value` folded into `externalises-a-first-value`; new `externalises-a-live-first-value` and `devices-a-changed-value` | **moved** / **new** | No subject may describe conforming behaviour; a build reporting a held key's change as `device` had no subject failing it |
| The tabled revision the workshop's backlog called applier 0.17 is renamed 0.18 | **moved** (outside the standard) | This revision took the number |

What stays: a held key's change is `external`; `self_changing` keys as before; steward GA-LEASE-2 is
unchanged (a `device` change takes no lease). Left to the backlog: a plug whose power-on behaviour changes
its held `on` at the return still reads `external` and leases (any fix must keep a wall switch's flick
`external`); the steward's harness has no subject that a `device` change takes no lease (steward 0.13).

## Readers

Claude reader, 2 high, 6 medium, 6 low; a code review, 8 minor; reports kept in the workshop archive.

into round 2's brief (the `device` gloss, *Compatibility*, the id in the text, the negative subjects in
both directions, the power-on note, the steward's missing subject).

Round 2 (every first value as `device`): Kimi `k3`, 1 blocker, 4 high, 4 medium, 2 low; a no-context
Claude reader, 2 high, 4 medium, 4 low; a code review, 1 important (pre-existing), 5 minor. Fixed in the
fix round (commits 0ae6a053, a99ccc04, d2e1eb2c, 904488c1):
- the blocker: GA-EVT-1 against GA-APPLY-11, a first value in the late-ack window is the apply's;
- "held" defined: the last value a bridge reported in the applier's current run, never an assumed value
  or `unknown` (the author's ruling: `unknown` reads `device` and clears the held value), kept through a
  status leaving the key out, `dead`, a broker loss, a new instance, a route move and a re-interview,
  dropped by a restart, `forget`, removal and an adoption with `replaces`;
- the cost stated both ways (a never-held key meeting a person's first act; a power-on behaviour
  changing a held key), with the checks on a real installation described in the standard itself and the
  ⚠️ moved out of the index row; the forged first value below Box;
- GA-META-6 relays the child's cause; an open-loop device's assumed values are never held; a value moving
  away from an apply's is `external` only for a held key;
- subjects: `devices-a-changed-value` excludes `self_changing` keys, covers a change at a return, and
  names GA-EVT-2 and GA-APPLY-11 as coupled; new `devices-a-correlated-first-value`;
- the build: a change judged against what the applier holds, not one bridge view's facts (the code
  review's important finding: a reinstalled bridge would have leased every device it re-reported); an
  unfolding copy's values taken as first values; the reconnection's snapshot answer restored;
- scenarios revision 18 (renumbered 19 at merge, after steward 0.13 took 18): HS3, HS8, HS18 and HS9 say "a key the applier holds"; HS3 gains the power-on case.
Deferred: guessed constants not declared in `describe` (Kimi, existing backlog entry); the steward subject
that a `device` change takes no lease (steward 0.13, taken by the steward session); a bridge reading an
adopted device's state after its start (bridge 0.7, backlog); a brain-standard note on `device` (backlog).
Rejected: keeping `external` for a live first value of a vocabulary key (the Claude reader): the
maintainer ruled every first value `device`.

Round 3: the no-context Claude reader alone (Kimi's quota was spent; the maintainer said to skip it, a
recorded exception to FR-REV-01's two readers for this round), no blocker, 2 high, 6 medium, 4 low; a
scoped code re-review, every round-2 code finding fixed, 3 minor. Fixed in the last fix round (commits
a3457c3f, d3b57f10, 5e76b630, a08ca377), not re-read by the stopping rule:
- `unknown` neither sets nor clears a held value (overruling the author's round-2 ruling, which let a
  `unknown` turn any held key's change into `device`);
- an adapter's engine is a source of held values;
- the costs: only the applier's own restart and downtime drop held values, a bridge's restart alone does
  not; a factory reset's defaults after a re-interview read `external`; a meta-applier's route move between
  children relays the new child's first value `device`;
- a first value during an apply is the apply's only where it matches within tolerance, or as a side
  effect (the author's ruling: a `set_level` showing `on` stays the apply's; neither takes a lease);
- the term "bridge view" removed; subjects graded across a broker loss and a new instance, and a restart
  bringing back a different value; an exposed id judged by what it holds; HS3's ⚠️ completed.
Deferred to applier 0.18 (the workshop's backlog): the late-ack window counted from a dead bridge's,
engine's or child's return; *Reissue*'s moving away after a coalesced report; a `setting_applied` change's
cause; a meta-applier holding values across children; GA-EVT-1 split into separate ids; the brain
standard's note on `device`.
Rejected: none this round.

## Scenario walk

Round 1: no grade moved; seven findings, the restart and short-outage ones leading to round 2's rule.
Round 2: no grade moved; six findings, folded into the fix round (held defined; costs; scenarios 18).
Round 3: no grade moved; seven findings: three fixed (adapters, the restart costs, HS3's ⚠️), four
deferred (the late-ack window after a return, *Reissue*, a setting's cause, the meta-applier's route move).

## Disposition

Every finding of the three rounds is fixed, deferred with where it is tracked, or rejected with its reason,
as listed under *Readers* and *Scenario walk*; the full reports are kept in the workshop archive.

## Verdict

**PASS**, 2026-10-13: pass 1 green on the final text; both readers in rounds 1 and 2, the Claude reader
alone in round 3 by the maintainer's word; a scenario walk each round, no grade moved; round 3 had no
blocker, so its fixes were not re-read (the stopping rule); no blocker open.
